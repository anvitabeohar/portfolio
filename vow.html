Project overview

Book the venue of your dreams without being there

Weddings in India are a $130 billion market, considered the country's fourth-largest industry, yet the sector is largely unorganised. Families who can afford a wedding planner find them expensive, and everyone else is left to arrange things themselves.

◎

The product

Vow is a comprehensive and user-friendly tool that empowers couples to plan and organise their dream wedding with ease.

!

The problem

People need a way to find and book wedding venues remotely.

★

The goal

Let users easily view and book their desired venue, with virtual tours and comprehensive venue information at their fingertips.

User research

Summary

I started by talking to 5 people in my network. When recruiting, I chose people of different ages and different relationships to the bride or groom, so the findings would generalise better. I kept the interview questions open so participants could talk freely about their past experience of organising a wedding. At this stage it mattered most to stay unbiased and open, so that I could spot the pain points.

Read the interview document ↗

Pain points

Price

Prices are unclear and change with demand.

Availability

It's hard to check whether a venue is free, and some are booked a year in advance. Several events at the same venue at once spoil the experience.

Visualisation

It's hard to picture how the venue will look on the day, and hard to communicate your own ideas.

Custom service

Settings are hard to customise. Venues usually offer fixed options and you can only choose from those.

Features

Deciding what each page does

With the information architecture mapped out, I listed the features each page needed, keeping accessibility in mind throughout.

  • Onboarding: sign up with Google, Facebook or phone; a tentative wedding date (or skip); location preference; Bride, Groom or other; and accessibility needs such as a screen reader or font size
  • Home: a personalised feed with filters (location, price range, open or closed, capacity), sorting by budget or popularity, a planning progress chart, schedules and vendor widgets
  • Venue page: pictures with an AR/VR view, rating, price range, description, capacity and hall types, wishlist, scheduling a call or visit, and audio descriptions for screen readers
  • Wishlist: compare venues' main features side by side, then schedule a call or book
Paper wireframes

Sketching five ways to solve each screen

Moving into the design phase, I brought together everything I had gathered so far: the personas, their pain points and the user flows. I sketched about 5 options for each screen, which let me explore different ways to include every feature and find the best approach.

Digital wireframes · Venue page

Seeing a venue without visiting it

When booking a wedding venue online, people weigh up how it looks, what it costs and which amenities it offers. To address this, I added a 360° virtual tour of the venue and the option to schedule a live call with the venue for a personal tour and any other questions.

  • A way to browse photos of each hall, room and garden
  • 360° virtual tour of the venue
  • Schedule a call with the venue
Digital wireframes · Planner

One place for every appointment

Planning a wedding can be overwhelming, especially keeping track of dozens of appointments that happen at the same time. To make this simpler, I created a planner page that manages every appointment and keeps track of all the bookings.

  • A wedding planning roadmap with a countdown
  • A calendar to track bookings and add and manage your own schedule

Usability study

Findings

Once the prototype was finished, Vow went through two rounds of usability testing.

Round 1 findings

  1. Use calendar booking.
  2. Add a burger menu and action bar for easier navigation.
  3. Create a dedicated view where customers can easily see and compare price changes for each hall in the venue.

Round 2 findings

  1. Let users create a profile in their account, rather than only identifying as the Bride or Groom.
  2. Add a messaging option for users who aren't comfortable phoning the venue, so they can send questions or concerns by text.

Visuals

Inspirational, not commanding

For the visual style I chose an inspirational approach rather than a commanding one, so the visuals support the content instead of competing with it. The mood board focused on simplicity and normcore aesthetics: mostly black and white, with subtle touches of accent colour.

Orange
#F08400 Peach

I chose orange because it holds deep cultural significance in India and is associated with happiness. Blue stands for loyalty and trust.
