Problem

Customers of IBM's Maximo adopt one capability and stop. Field Service Management's value is hard to see from where they stand.

What I did

Wrote the expert interview guide, ran 7 interviews across sales, product and renewals, mapped the customer journey and its adoption barriers, and built a recommender proof of concept.

Context

IBM's Asset Lifecycle Management (ALM) is built around Maximo, an enterprise asset management product with around 40 years of history. I joined Expansion, a new initiative helping existing and prospective customers see the value of adopting adjacent ALM capabilities. The first focus was Field Service Management (FSM).

The question wasn't “why is this hard to use?” It was “why don't customers who already rely on Maximo take the next step?”

My role

  • Research design: wrote the subject-matter expert (SME) interview guide.
  • Interviews and synthesis: ran 7 interviews and turned them into a customer journey map of adoption barriers.
  • Prototyping: built a recommender proof of concept with IBM's internal AI tooling.

Research

I wrote the interview guide in two layers:

  • The experts' own view: where SMEs themselves struggle to position FSM.
  • The customer's view: the pain points SMEs see in the customers they work with.

I interviewed 7 people across product management, sales and renewals, covering EMEA and North America.

The journey map

I mapped the customer journey and marked where adoption stalls. The map is now also part of the FSM knowledge base.

From map to model

My manager and a lead UX researcher developed my journey map into a five-stage ALM maturity model, from a base level to the most optimised. The model is theirs; the map it was built on is mine.

Workshop

In June we ran a two-day workshop with 15–16 people, nearly all executives, plus 2 from sales.

Recommender proof of concept

I built a recommender proof of concept with IBM's internal AI tooling. Users answer multiple-choice questions and are placed across four areas, including planning and scheduling. Its primary users are sales and product managers, with a path into the product itself.

Where it is now

  • The recommender proof of concept is live internally.
  • The journey map is part of the FSM knowledge base.
  • The team grew from 4 to 13 and split into two teams through an internal challenge. I moved to the knowledge base side; the other team carried the recommender forward.
  • ALM's focus has since shifted to Asset Performance Management (APM), with FSM as the second priority.
