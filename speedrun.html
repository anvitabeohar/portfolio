Designing for AI

People should understand, trust and control what AI does for them

As AI starts reasoning and acting on people's behalf, the design problem shifts from what it can do to how people understand, trust and steer it. I design around four questions:

Show your work
What is it doing, and why? Make activity, reasoning, sources and confidence visible.
Let me drive
Can I scope it, stop it or approve it? Ask before consequential actions, and let people set how much autonomy it has.
Help me fix it
Can I recover? Edit, undo, try an alternative, and give feedback with context.
Set expectations
What can it do, and what happens at the edges? Be honest about limits and show partial success, so trust is earned, not blind.

I also match the interface to the job: a quick micro-task, a creative companion, a delegated workflow or an ambient teammate each need different levels of visibility and control.

LVL 02 · Research · VR

Is VR esports Olympic-ready?

Question
Olympic VR events mostly copy real sports. Can a VR-native game meet Olympic standards for exertion, skill and safety?
My role
Sole researcher on my MSc thesis: study design, ethics approval, recruitment, sessions, analysis and write-up.
What I did
Ran a 29-person lab study of Beat Saber 360° on Meta Quest 3, measuring heart rate, perceived effort and VR sickness over four sessions.
Impact
Average heart rate of 114–124 bpm, well above resting (p < .001). Six design principles for VR-native sports. Awarded with Distinction.
